Where Are
the Big Dogs?

Big cats dominate our myths, movies, mascots, and imaginations. But giant dogs once occupied North America too. This page documents the evidence behind the speech: what those animals were, why their lineages disappeared, and why we barely remember them.

A source page for an informational speech on extinct canids, competition with felids, and the cultural visibility of charismatic animals.

02 / Specimens

Two animals worth knowing.

Canid 01

Epicyon haydeni

The Florida Museum describes Epicyon as the largest known canid. It belonged to Borophaginae, an extinct North American group commonly called the “bone-crushing dogs.”

Paleoart: A. James Gustafson.

Canid 02

Borophagus

A late bone-crushing canid with powerful jaws. Fossilized feces containing bone fragments provide unusually direct evidence that these animals regularly consumed bone.

Image: U.S. National Park Service / Reid Psaltis.

Mascot note

Among the 20 current SWAC and MEAC member schools, seven use wild-cat identities and none use a wild-canid identity. Two use Bulldogs. The contrast is used in the speech as a small example of how culturally visible cats remain.
